Tankless Water Heater No Hot Water: A Douglasville Homeowner Guide

A tankless heater that stops making hot water is usually blocked, starved or scaled, not dead. These checks take minutes and tell you which.

Call (770) 214-4545

By the Douglasville 24/7 Plumbing team · 2026.

When a tankless water heater delivers only cold or lukewarm water, the cause is almost always one of five things. They are no fuel or power, a blocked vent, a clogged inlet screen, a scaled heat exchanger or a flow too low to fire the burner. The unit's display often shows a code that narrows it down.

Work through the list below from the simplest check to the hardest. If you smell gas at any point, leave the house and call the gas utility or 911 first. For a unit that is leaking, close the water to it and call our 24/7 line.

The five-minute checks

Start here before calling

These checks need no tools and cost nothing.

  1. 1

    Check power and the display. Is the unit lit, and is there an error code? Write it down.

  2. 2

    Check the gas supply. Make sure the gas valve is open and other gas appliances work. A closed valve or empty propane tank is a common cause.

  3. 3

    Check the water valves. Both the cold inlet and hot outlet valves should be fully open.

  4. 4

    Open a different tap. If only one fixture is cold, the problem is a mixing valve or cartridge there and not the heater.

  5. 5

    Look at the vent. Birds, ice or debris at the exhaust can shut a unit down for safety.

Why a tankless unit gives lukewarm water

Cause, sign and fix

Most of these are service items. A tankless unit that has not been flushed in years is the usual culprit.

The cold-water sandwich and crossover problems

A short burst of cold between two bursts of hot is a known behavior on many tankless units, because the heater shuts off and restarts as demand changes. It is a nuisance, and it gets worse when flow is low.

Crossover is a different issue. If hot and cold lines are connected somewhere, water can mix before it reaches the fixture, and the heater may not see enough flow to fire. A plumber finds it by isolating fixtures one at a time.

When descaling is the answer

Mineral scale narrows the heat exchanger's passages and insulates it, so the unit works harder and then shuts down. A descaling flush circulates a mild acid solution through the unit and rinses it clean. Units with isolation valves can be flushed in a single visit, and the tankless water heater page explains what a flush includes.

If your unit does not have service valves, they can be added at the same visit, which makes every later flush simpler.

When to call a plumber right away

  • Any smell of gas.
  • Water leaking from the unit or its connections.
  • Repeated error codes after a reset.
  • No hot water for several days after the checks above.
  • Carbon monoxide alarm sounding near the unit.

A carbon monoxide alarm means leave the house and call 911. Vent problems on a gas unit are a safety matter and not a plumbing inconvenience.

What a tankless service visit includes

A proper service visit starts with the error history on the display, then moves through the gas supply pressure, the combustion check, the vent and the water side. The inlet screen is cleaned, the heat exchanger is flushed if scale is present, and the temperature sensors are checked against the display.

You should leave with a plain explanation of what was found and a flush schedule that fits your water. If the plumber suggests a part, ask to see the failed one and to hear why it failed.

Repair or replace a tankless unit

Many tankless units last around 20 years with maintenance, so a unit under ten years old with a single failed part is nearly always worth repairing. A unit with a cracked heat exchanger, or one that has not been flushed in many years and keeps failing, is a different story. The water heater installation page explains the choices if you decide to replace it.

Douglasville: what a tankless repair touches locally

The Douglasville-Douglas County Water and Sewer Authority's report does not list water hardness, which is why testing your tap is the first step before you pick a descaling schedule. Homes in Lithia Springs and the nearby towns on our service areas page follow the same advice.

Georgia's state minimum plumbing code is the International Plumbing Code (IPC) with Georgia amendments, enforced locally. Tankless units also follow the maker's instructions, so keep the manual with your records. Homes in Lithia Springs follow the same rules.
