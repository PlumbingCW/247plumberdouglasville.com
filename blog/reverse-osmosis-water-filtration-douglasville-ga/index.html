What Is a Reverse Osmosis Water Filtration System? Douglasville Guide

Reverse osmosis pushes water through a membrane so fine it blocks most dissolved solids. It is a drinking water filter, not a whole-house cure.

Call (770) 214-4545

By the Douglasville 24/7 Plumbing team · 2026.

A reverse osmosis system, usually called RO, filters drinking water by forcing it through a semipermeable membrane. The membrane lets water molecules pass and blocks most dissolved solids, which end up in a waste stream that goes down the drain. The result is a small flow of very clean water at one tap.

Most home systems are installed under the kitchen sink with a separate faucet on the counter. This guide explains how they work, what they do well, what they cost in water and maintenance, and what to ask before buying one. Whole-house treatment is a different question, which we cover at the end.

How a typical RO system works

From the tap to the glass

Most under-sink units use a series of stages.

  1. 1

    Sediment pre-filter. It catches sand, rust and particles that could damage the membrane.

  2. 2

    Carbon pre-filter. It removes chlorine and some organic compounds, which can harm the membrane.

  3. 3

    RO membrane. It blocks dissolved solids and many contaminants, and sends them to the drain.

  4. 4

    Storage tank. Because RO is slow, treated water collects in a small tank until you open the faucet.

  5. 5

    Post-filter. A final carbon filter polishes the taste before water reaches the dedicated faucet.

What an RO system costs to run

  • Filters need replacing on a schedule, with the pre-filters and post-filter replaced more often than the membrane.
  • The membrane lasts longer than the filters but needs replacing eventually.
  • The system sends water to the drain while it works, so ask the maker about the waste ratio.
  • Some units need a booster pump if household water pressure is low.
  • The storage tank needs its air charge checked.

Skipping filter changes defeats the purpose, because a spent pre-filter can let chlorine reach the membrane and ruin it.

Do you need one

Start with a test. A basic home test kit or a lab test tells you what is in your tap water, and the utility's annual water quality report tells you what the utility measures. RO solves specific problems, and the test shows if you have them.

If the concern is taste, a carbon pitcher or a simple carbon filter may do. If the concern is a specific contaminant, check that the system is certified for it. If the concern is hard water scale on fixtures and the water heater, a softener is the tool and RO is not.

Installation notes

A plumber mounts the unit under the sink, adds a feed valve on the cold line, runs a drain connection above the trap and drills the sink or counter for the faucet. The drain connection must have an air gap or be installed to prevent backflow. Our plumbing installation page explains how we handle planned fixture work.

If an existing disposal or dishwasher shares the space, the layout matters. Allow room for the tank and for changing the filters.

Upkeep that keeps an RO system working

Reverse osmosis systems depend on filters that wear out. The sediment and carbon pre-filters protect the membrane, and the membrane itself lasts longer when the pre-filters are changed on time. Follow the maker's schedule and write the dates on the housing.

A drop in flow, a change in taste or a tank that fills slowly are signs it is time for service. Sanitizing the system when filters are changed keeps the storage tank clean.

Where the drain line goes

An RO system sends rejected water to a drain, usually through an air gap faucet or a saddle valve on the sink drain. The connection has to be tight and positioned so wastewater cannot siphon back. A gurgling sound after the tank refills can mean the drain connection needs attention.

Douglasville: start with the utility's report

The Douglasville-Douglas County Water and Sewer Authority treats surface water from the Dog River Reservoir at its Bear Creek plant and publishes a yearly water quality report. The 2026 report does not list a hardness figure, so test your tap and ask the authority for details before you size a treatment system.

Georgia licenses plumbers through the Division of Master and Journeyman Plumbers, and the Douglas County building department can tell you whether a new fixture connection needs a permit. The county's permit form lists sinks and lavatories as fee items, and homes in Lithia Springs follow the same county office.

Georgia's state minimum plumbing code is the International Plumbing Code (IPC) with Georgia amendments, enforced locally. Protection against backflow applies to connections such as the RO drain line, which is why the air gap matters.
