Plumbing Installation in Douglasville, GA

New fixtures, kitchen and bath remodels, additions and gas connections, planned and installed to code.

Plumbing installation covers the planned work that is not a repair: a new bathroom, a kitchen remodel, a laundry hookup, a sink in a new room, a new water line or a gas connection. The work is done in a set order, so each step is inspected before it is closed up. Plumbing installation in Douglasville, GA starts with a walk-through and a clear price.

What we install

  • Sinks, lavatories, tubs, showers and toilets.
  • Kitchen sinks, dishwashers and disposal connections.
  • Laundry boxes, washer drains and utility sinks.
  • Outdoor spigots and hose bibs.
  • Water lines to additions, workshops and outbuildings.
  • Gas appliance connections, with safety checks.

Water heaters and tankless units have their own pages. See water heater installation and tankless water heaters.

Planning a bathroom or kitchen remodel

The best plumbing plan begins before the walls are open. A plumber should see the drawings, the fixture list and the location of drains and vents, because moving a toilet or a tub changes more than the fixture. Drains must slope, vents must reach the roof or connect properly, and pipe sizes must match the load.

Gas lines and appliance connections

A gas connection is safety work. If you smell gas, leave the building and call the gas utility or 911 before you call a plumber. For planned work, a gas line is sized for the total load, tested for leaks and inspected where required.

Douglas County's residential plumbing permit application includes gas lines among the items it lists, so a new gas connection is a job to confirm with the building department.

Permits and inspections

Many installs need a permit. Douglas County's building department is at 4655 Timber Ridge Drive, and its residential plumbing permit application lists fixtures, water heaters, gas lines, sump pumps, backflow preventers and underground water and sewer lines. Rules vary by project and address, so call before work starts.

The application asks for the plumbing contractor's state card number. Georgia's Division of Master and Journeyman Plumbers issues the state license, and only a licensed master plumber may operate a plumbing business, so ask who holds the master license.

Other systems customers ask about

Homeowners also ask about water softeners, filtration systems, sump pumps, backflow preventers and new construction rough-ins. We can advise on the plumbing side of each when they are part of an install. Backflow preventers should be tested as your local authority requires, so ask the Douglasville-Douglas County Water and Sewer Authority what applies to your property.

For new construction, the plumbing is roughed in before the walls close, then finished after drywall. Coordination with the builder, the inspector and the other trades is part of the job.

Code and permits for new work

Georgia's state minimum plumbing code is the International Plumbing Code (IPC) with Georgia amendments, enforced locally. New fixtures and rough-ins are expected to meet it, and the county's residential plumbing permit form lists the items that carry fees.
