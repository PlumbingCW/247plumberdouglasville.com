A shop, restaurant or office with a plumbing failure loses money by the hour. The line is answered 24/7.

For a business, a plumbing problem is a business problem. A backed-up restroom or a leaking line in a kitchen can close the doors, and the faster it is fixed, the less it costs. Our line is open 24/7, and a licensed plumber can be dispatched after hours, on weekends and on holidays.

If you manage a building, shut off the water at the nearest isolation valve for the affected area, or at the main, and call. Keep people away from wet floors and anything that is wet near electrical equipment.

Business plumbing differs from a home's in volume, layout and code. Larger pipes, more fixtures, shared systems and tenant spaces change how a job is planned. Our plumbers confirm the scope and the property owner's rules before work begins.

Restaurants and food service

Kitchens produce grease, and grease builds up in lines even when a trap is in place. A scheduled hydro jetting visit is cheaper than a Friday-night backup. Grease trap cleaning, floor drains and handsinks are part of keeping a kitchen open, and your inspector will expect them to work.

Several commercial districts around Douglasville, including the Douglas Boulevard area near Arbor Place and downtown near O'Neal Plaza, have food service tenants. Each building's piping is different, so we look at yours.

Backflow testing, grease traps and storm drains

Many commercial properties have backflow preventers that protect the public water supply, and some must be tested on a regular schedule. Ask the Douglasville-Douglas County Water and Sewer Authority what applies to your property, and keep the test records. Douglas County's residential permit application lists backflow preventers as a fee item, which gives an idea of how they are treated.

Grease traps need regular cleaning. Storm drains on a commercial lot can clog with leaves, sediment and trash, and a blocked storm drain floods a parking lot fast. Ask us about a clearing schedule for your property.

Leak detection in a commercial building

Commercial leak calls are usually a puzzle of hidden lines, many tenants and a large meter. A leak detection visit uses the same tools as in a home, with more area to cover. A written report helps when a landlord and tenant need to agree on who pays.

Be aware that commercial leak work is often a larger scope than owners expect, and a clear scope up front avoids surprises. Our plumbers explain the plan before any work.

Code points for commercial work

Georgia's state minimum plumbing code is the International Plumbing Code (IPC) with Georgia amendments, enforced locally. Commercial buildings add requirements of their own, such as fixture counts and grease control, so confirm what applies to your occupancy with the local building office.
