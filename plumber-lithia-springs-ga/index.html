Who handles permits and utilities here

Lithia Springs is a census-designated place, not an incorporated city, which means Douglas County government covers it. For plumbing, that points you to the county building department for permits and inspections. Ask before a water heater replacement, a new gas line or any underground water or sewer line work.

The Cobb County line forms the community's northern and eastern edges. Homes close to it may sit on a different utility's map than their neighbors, so read your bill. Knowing the provider tells you who is responsible for the pipe from the street to the meter and who to call for a main break.

A town named for its water

The community takes its name from the lithia mineral springs, which drew visitors in the late 1800s. A resort hotel opened there in 1887, and the springs were bottled and sold. Today the municipal water comes from the Dog River Reservoir through the Bear Creek plant, so the old springs have nothing to do with what comes out of your tap.

The practical lesson is to ask about your own water. The authority's report lists no hardness figure, so a strip test at the tap tells you whether to flush your water heater yearly, and see water heater repair if the tank is already rumbling.

Plumbing work here is held to Georgia's state minimum plumbing code, the International Plumbing Code (IPC) with Georgia amendments. The county enforces it, so a permitted job in Lithia Springs is inspected by the county building department.

Near Sweetwater Creek State Park

Sweetwater Creek State Park, with its reservoir and the ruins of the Manchester Mill, is the best-known landmark here. For a homeowner nearby, the creek valley means sloped lots where a drain line has to hold its grade. A line that sags collects water and clogs, which is when a camera look is useful, see sewer camera inspection.

Clay soil in this part of the Piedmont swells and shrinks, and it moves buried pipe joints with it. Repeated clogs are the sign, and drain cleaning clears the immediate problem while the camera finds the cause.
