Emergency Water Line Repair in Douglasville, GA

The pipe that feeds your home runs underground, and when it fails, the signs show up in your yard and on your bill.

Call (770) 214-4545

Your water service line is the buried pipe that carries water from the meter to your house. When it breaks, you may see a soggy patch in the yard, hear water running, notice low pressure or watch the meter spin while the house is off. A bad break is an emergency, and the line is answered 24/7.

While you wait, close the main valve at the house. If water is still flowing in the yard, the leak is upstream of that valve, and the valve at the meter is the next to close. Do not dig near a buried line before calling the 811 utility locator.

Signs of a service line leak that cannot wait

  • A wet, sunken or greener patch of yard with no rain or sprinkler explanation.
  • A meter that moves with the house shut off.
  • Dirty or discolored water.
  • Low water pressure throughout the house.
  • A hissing sound near the meter or at the house entry.

Who owns what

In general, the property owner is responsible for the pipe on the house side of the meter. The Douglasville-Douglas County Water and Sewer Authority handles the system up to and including the meter, but ask the authority where the line of responsibility sits at your address.

That matters when you call a plumber. We locate the leak with acoustic tools, tell you which side of the meter it is on, and recommend the next step.

Repair methods for a service line

The right method depends on the pipe material, the length, the depth and what sits above it.

MethodWhen it fitsWhat to expect.
Spot dig and repairOne clear leak in sound pipeA small excavation and a short section replaced.
Open trench replacementOld or heavily corroded lineA trench along the line, restoration of the surface.
Trenchless replacementLayout and soil allow itSmaller access pits at each end.
Pressure regulator replacementHigh or erratic pressureOften done with the line repair.

Low water pressure

Low pressure has several causes. A partly closed valve, a failed pressure regulator, a clogged aerator, corroded galvanized pipe inside the house or a leak in the service line can all do it. We start with a gauge at an outside hose bib, which tells us whether the problem is the supply or something inside the house.

If the gauge reads low and the meter spins when the house is off, the leak is likely buried. If it reads normal and only some fixtures are weak, the cause is inside, and may point to repiping or a single clogged fixture.

Replacing a service line for new work

Some jobs are not repairs at all. A remodel, an addition or a new outbuilding may need a larger line, or a new one. Our plumbing installation page covers planned work, and a line sized for the new load is part of that plan.

What affects the cost of water line repair

You get a clear price before work starts. The factors are these.

Length and depthLonger, deeper lines take more digging or more access pits.
Pipe materialCopper, PEX and HDPE each have different costs and lifespans.
What is above itDriveways, sidewalks and landscaping add restoration work.
MethodSpot repair, trench and trenchless are priced differently.
Permit feesDouglas County's 2025 residential plumbing permit application prices underground water service lines per foot, so a longer run adds to the permit.

Georgia soil is mostly clay in this part of the Piedmont, and clay holds water and shifts with the weather. That movement is one reason older lines crack, and it is why we check the line's grade and bedding when it is exposed.

Code points for the service line

Georgia's state minimum plumbing code is the International Plumbing Code (IPC) with Georgia amendments, enforced locally. It covers water service piping, materials and depth in general terms, and the county permit form prices underground water service lines by the foot.
