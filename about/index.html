About Douglasville 24/7 Plumbing

A dispatch line for plumbing emergencies and planned work in Douglasville and nearby towns.

Call (770) 214-4545

Douglasville 24/7 Plumbing is a dispatch service for plumbing work in Douglasville, GA and the towns around it. You call one number, the line is answered at any hour, and a licensed plumber is sent to your address. The business address is 3696 Highway 5, Douglasville, GA 30135.

What happens when you call

The call, step by step

The process is simple, and every part of it is meant to get water and drains under control quickly.

  1. 1

    We ask where you are and what you see. The dispatcher takes your address, the symptom and what you have already shut off.

  2. 2

    We tell you what to do while you wait. Closing a valve or stopping drain use can save a floor.

  3. 3

    A licensed plumber is dispatched. Same-day appointments are available when a slot is open.

  4. 4

    You get a clear price before work starts. If the job changes on site, the plumber explains why before going further.

  5. 5

    The work is done and explained. You are told what was found, what was done and what to watch.

How we describe our plumbers

The work is done by licensed plumbers. Georgia licenses plumbers through the Division of Master and Journeyman Plumbers, and only a licensed master plumber may operate a plumbing business. Ask for the license holder's details on any job, ours or anyone else's, and confirm them with the Division.

We do not publish reviews, ratings or years-in-business figures on this site, because we want everything you read to be something you can check. That includes the local facts, which come from sources such as the Douglasville-Douglas County Water and Sewer Authority and the Douglas County building department.

What we do not do

  • We do not promise an arrival time. We tell you when a plumber is dispatched and give you an honest window.
  • We do not quote a fixed price over the phone for a job we have not seen. We give a clear price before work starts.
  • We do not handle every service. Water softener, filtration, sump pump and septic work are not covered by pages on this site yet, and the dispatcher will say if we can help.

Where we work

Douglasville, Lithia Springs, Austell, Hiram, Villa Rica, Winston and Mableton. See the service areas page for how county lines affect permits, and the services page for what we dispatch for.

How the site handles local facts

Where a page names a local fact, such as where the water comes from or what the county permit form lists, it comes from a published source and the page says which one. Where no source exists, the page tells you how to check, for example by testing your own tap for hardness.

That is why some questions on these pages end with a suggestion to ask the authority instead of a number. A missing number is better than a guessed one, especially for anything involving safety, code or cost.

What to expect from a first call

The first call is short. The dispatcher confirms your address, asks what you see and tells you what to shut off. If the situation is an emergency, a plumber is dispatched while you are still on the phone. If it is planned work, you are offered the next open appointment.

You do not need to know the cause of the problem. Describe the symptom, such as a wet ceiling, a slow main drain or a tank that is cold, and the plumber works out the cause on site.

Why we publish what we do not do

A plumbing page that promises everything is easy to write and hard to trust. We would rather tell you where the limits are. If a job is outside what we dispatch for, you hear that on the first call, and you can look elsewhere without losing a day.

The same approach applies to prices. A price given before work starts is a price you can compare, and a price that changes on site comes with a reason from the plumber before anything else is done.

The standard the work is held to

Georgia's state minimum plumbing code is the International Plumbing Code (IPC) with Georgia amendments, and local governments enforce it. A permitted job is inspected against it. When you compare plumbers, ask how they handle permits and inspections.

What a good plumbing visit looks like

A good visit starts with the plumber listening to what you saw and heard. Then comes a look at the fixture, the pipe or the equipment, and an explanation in plain words. You should know what is wrong before you agree to anything.

At the end, the plumber tells you what was done, what to watch for and when to call again. Keep that conversation in mind when you compare any plumbing company.

Who the services are for

  • Homeowners with a leak, a backup or no hot water.
  • Landlords and property managers with a tenant waiting on a repair.
  • Owners of shops, restaurants and small offices with after-hours problems.
  • Buyers who want a sewer line or plumbing system checked before closing.

Call once, get a plan, and know the price before the work begins.
