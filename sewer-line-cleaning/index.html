Emergency Sewer Line Cleaning in Douglasville, GA

Main sewer lines clog from roots, grease, wipes and sagging pipe. We clean them out and show you what caused it.

Call (770) 214-4545

When the main sewer line is blocked, every drain in the house is affected. Water has nowhere to go, and the lowest fixture in the house, often a basement shower, tub or floor drain, is where the backup shows first. This is an emergency, and our line is open 24/7 to dispatch a plumber.

While you wait, stop all water use in the house, keep people out of any wet area, and do not flush toilets. Every flush adds more water to a line that cannot take it.

Emergency main line blockage: what blocks a sewer line

Most main line clogs are not a single event. They are a slow build of grease, paper and debris that narrow the pipe until something small closes it. Tree roots, flushed wipes and pipe offsets turn a narrow pipe into a blocked one.

How a main line cleaning works

The sequence from the cleanout

Work starts at the cleanout, not the toilet, whenever possible.

  1. 1

    Find the cleanout. A cleanout gives direct access to the line and is the safest entry point.

  2. 2

    Cable the blockage. A machine cable opens the line and clears the immediate stoppage.

  3. 3

    Scour if needed. Jetting cleans the pipe wall when the buildup is heavy.

  4. 4

    Look with a camera. The camera shows what remains and what caused the clog.

  5. 5

    Report and plan. You get the findings, any repair needs and a care plan.

Our hydro jetting page explains when scouring is the better choice than a cable, and the drain cleaning page covers branch lines that clear without a main line visit.

Sewer or septic: it changes the plan

Douglas County's residential plumbing permit application lists county sewer and septic tank as separate sewer types, and homes in the county are served either way. If your house is on county sewer, the cleaning clears the lateral to the connection. If it is on septic, a blocked outlet or a full tank may be the cause.

Tell the dispatcher which you have when you call. A septic system needs a different set of questions, and a tank pump-out is a separate service from a sewer line cleaning.

Keeping a cleaned line clean

  • Put only toilet paper and human waste in the toilet, never wipes, even ones marked flushable.
  • Collect cooking grease in a can and throw it out.
  • Keep large trees away from the line's path when planting.
  • Have a camera inspection if the line has clogged more than once in a year.
  • Know where your cleanout is and keep it accessible.

Why cleanouts matter

Georgia's state minimum plumbing code is the International Plumbing Code (IPC) with Georgia amendments, enforced locally. It calls for cleanouts in building drains, and good access is what lets a cable or jetter reach the blockage without opening the ground.
