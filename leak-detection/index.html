Leak emergency: the meter is running and nobody is using water

Most leak calls start with a symptom and not with the word leak. The meter keeps turning, the water bill is far higher than usual, a ceiling stain appears, or a floor feels warm. If water is actively coming through a ceiling or wall, shut the main valve, turn off power to the wet area and call. Our line is open 24/7 for emergencies like that.

For a slow suspect leak, you can run a check yourself in a few minutes. It tells you whether the leak is real and whether it sits before or after the house shutoff.

A two-minute meter test you can do now

Use this before you call, or while you wait for the plumber.

  1. 1

    Turn everything off. Close every faucet, stop the washer and dishwasher, and confirm the ice maker is not filling.

  2. 2

    Find the meter. It is usually in a box near the street or the property line. Lift the lid and look for the low-flow indicator, often a small triangle or dial.

  3. 3

    Watch for motion. If the indicator spins, water is moving through the system.

  4. 4

    Close the house main. If the indicator stops, the leak is inside the house. If it keeps moving, the leak is between the meter and the house.

  5. 5

    Call with the result. Tell the dispatcher what you saw, because it focuses the search.

The Douglasville-Douglas County Water and Sewer Authority supplies water in the county, and it can tell you how to read your meter and how to compare your usage month to month. A plumber uses that information to narrow the search.

Ceiling, wall and attic leaks

Water follows gravity and framing, so the stain is rarely where the leak is. A drip on a ceiling may start at a joint in the attic and run along a joist before it shows. We use a moisture meter and a thermal camera to trace the wet area back to its source before any drywall is cut.

That keeps repairs small. It also gives you documentation for your insurer, who will ask where the water came from and for how long.

Under-house and crawlspace leaks

Crawlspace pipes sit out of sight, and a slow leak can soak the insulation and subfloor for months. Signs include a musty smell, sagging insulation, bouncy floors and a higher bill. Our plumbers check supply lines, drain connections and the water heater for the source.

Buried lines, yard leaks and sprinklers

A leak in the line between the meter and the house is invisible until the ground softens or the bill jumps. Acoustic listening picks up the sound of water escaping from a pressurized pipe, and a locator marks the spot so the repair is a small dig. For the repair itself, see water line repair.

Irrigation systems leak at valves, risers and buried lines. If your system runs on a timer, a stuck valve can run through the night and show up only on the bill. Pool and spa plumbing leaks are tested separately, and we can advise on the supply side.

A slab leak is a special case of a hidden leak under concrete, and has its own page, slab leak repair.

Why pressure tests follow a standard

Georgia's state minimum plumbing code is the International Plumbing Code (IPC) with Georgia amendments, enforced locally. Pressure and leak testing of plumbing is a recognized step in it, which is why a detection visit often starts with isolating and testing each system.
