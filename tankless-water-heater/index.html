Tankless Water Heaters in Douglasville, GA: Install, Repair and Flush

Wall-mounted units for homes that want endless hot water, with the yearly flush that keeps them running.

Call (770) 214-4545

A tankless water heater heats water only when you open a tap, which is why it never runs out the way a tank does. It also means the unit has to heat a lot of water very fast, so gas line size, venting and flow rate matter more than the label. Tankless water heaters in Douglasville, GA are where our plumbers spend time on the details.

How a tankless unit works, and what it asks of your house

Cold water enters, a flow sensor tells the burner or elements to fire, and the unit raises the temperature as the water passes through the heat exchanger. The unit can only raise water so many degrees at a given flow, so running a shower, a dishwasher and a washer at once can exceed it.

Gas units often need a larger gas line than the tank they replace, plus stainless or approved venting to the outside. Electric units draw heavy current and may need a new breaker and heavier wire. The first visit checks those two things before anything is ordered.

Tank versus tankless

The comparison is about fit. A tank is a good answer for a tight budget and a short timeline, and a tankless unit suits a house with room for the equipment.

QuestionTank heaterTankless heater.
Hot water supplyLimited to the tank volumeContinuous, up to the unit's flow limit.
Floor spaceTakes a closet or garage cornerMounts on the wall.
Upfront costLowerHigher, plus any gas or venting upgrade.
MaintenanceDrain the tank and check the anode rodFlush and descale the heat exchanger.
Typical service lifeAbout 8 to 12 yearsOften 20 years with maintenance.

Flushing and descaling: the job most owners skip

Minerals in the water coat the inside of the heat exchanger over time. A coated exchanger heats less efficiently, throws error codes and eventually fails early. A flush circulates a descaling solution through the unit and then rinses it out.

Units installed with service valves can be flushed in a single visit. Units without them need valves added first, which we recommend at install. How often you flush depends on your water, and the Douglasville-Douglas County Water and Sewer Authority's water quality report does not publish a hardness figure, so test your tap and ask the authority for its latest numbers before you pick a schedule.

No hot water, lukewarm water and error codes

The common causes are a clogged inlet filter screen, a scaled heat exchanger, a gas supply problem, a blocked vent, or a flow rate too low to trigger the burner. Many units display a code that points to the section of the system at fault.

The cold-water sandwich, a short blast of cold between two bursts of hot, is normal on many tankless models. It becomes a service issue when it gets worse or the unit shuts down mid-shower.

How a tankless install is planned

The work is done in a set order so the first hot shower is a safe one.

  1. 1

    Survey. We check gas line size, vent path, water pressure, and the electrical panel for an electric unit.

  2. 2

    Select. The unit is chosen for your fixtures, the incoming water temperature and your flow demand.

  3. 3

    Prepare. Gas, venting, condensate drain and service valves are put in place.

  4. 4

    Mount and connect. The unit goes on the wall with isolation valves so future flushes are easy.

  5. 5

    Test. Flow, temperature and combustion are checked and the settings are explained.

Where code meets a tankless install

Georgia's state minimum plumbing code is the International Plumbing Code (IPC) with Georgia amendments, enforced locally. A tankless install touches it through the water connections and relief devices, while gas and venting have their own rules, so ask the plumber how each is handled.
