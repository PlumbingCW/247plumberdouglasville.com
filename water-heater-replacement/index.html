Signs your water heater is past repair

A tank that has started leaking from its body is finished. Water at the base that returns after you dry it, rust in the hot water, or a heater older than 10 to 12 years that keeps needing parts all point the same way. Replacing sooner costs less than repairing twice and then replacing anyway.

This is also the page for the emergency version of that problem. If the tank is already flooding a garage or closet, close the cold supply valve above it, turn off the power at the breaker or set the gas control to pilot, and call. Our line is open 24/7, so the call does not have to wait for morning.

Gas smell means leave and call the gas utility or 911 first. After that, call us.

Why tanks fail when they do

Inside every steel tank is a glass lining and an anode rod. The rod corrodes on purpose so the steel does not. Once it is consumed, the tank starts to rust from the inside, and a pinhole leak follows.

Sediment speeds that up. Minerals settle on the bottom, trap heat, and bake against the steel. Pressure swings finish the job, especially in homes with a closed system and no expansion tank. If your relief valve drips after every heating cycle, that is a sign worth mentioning to the plumber.

Tank, tankless or hybrid: choosing the next heater

Most emergency swaps stay with the same fuel and size to get hot water back fast. The table is for when you have time to choose.

What replacement day looks like

The job follows the same order whether it is planned or an emergency.

  1. 1

    Shut off and drain. Water, power or gas are isolated and the old tank is drained.

  2. 2

    Remove and haul away. The old heater is disconnected and carried out so the space is clear.

  3. 3

    Set and connect. The new heater goes on a drain pan when the location needs one, with new flex connectors and shutoff valves.

  4. 4

    Check the safety parts. Relief valve, discharge pipe, expansion tank, venting and gas connections are checked against the heater's instructions and local code.

  5. 5

    Fill, test and explain. The tank is filled and purged of air, the heating is tested, and you are shown the shutoff and the temperature setting.

Water damage after a failed tank

A tank that has burst can release dozens of gallons. Stop the water first, then photograph the area, move belongings out of the wet zone, and report the damage to your insurer. Policies tend to respond to sudden leaks and not to the age of the heater.

Drywall and insulation that stay wet for more than a day or two invite mold, so ask for the wet materials to be dried or removed early.

Hiring for a replacement in Douglasville

Georgia licenses plumbers through the Division of Master and Journeyman Plumbers. The Division does not register plumbing companies, and only a licensed master plumber may run a plumbing business, so ask who holds the master license for your job and that it is current.

Ask also for the model, the warranty length and what is included in the price. Your Douglasville home draws water that the Douglasville-Douglas County Water and Sewer Authority treats from the Dog River Reservoir. The authority's report does not publish hardness, so a strip test at your tap tells you whether a sediment-friendly maintenance plan is worth it.

Emergency water heater failure: what to do before the plumber arrives

  • Turn off power at the breaker or the gas at the heater's valve.
  • Close the cold water supply to the tank.
  • Do not touch the tank if it is leaking near electrical parts.
  • Move items away from the base.
  • Do not drain a very hot tank by hand.

The code side of a tank swap

Georgia's state minimum plumbing code is the International Plumbing Code (IPC) with Georgia amendments, enforced locally. A replacement tank is expected to meet it, including relief valve discharge and the connections, and the county permit form lists water heaters as an item.
