Emergency Toilet Repair in Douglasville, GA

An overflowing toilet or a leak around the base needs fast action. The line is open 24/7 for repairs, clogs and installs.

Call (770) 214-4545

A toilet that is overflowing, leaking onto the floor or refusing to flush is an emergency in a house with one bathroom. Close the shutoff valve behind the toilet by turning it clockwise, lift the tank lid and lift the flapper if water is still rising, and call. Our line is open 24/7, and a licensed plumber can be dispatched at any hour.

Do not keep flushing or plunging a toilet that is rising. Every flush sends more water into a bowl that cannot take it, and a clog that is past the toilet is a drain problem and not a toilet problem.

A running toilet and a high water bill

A toilet that runs or refills on its own can waste a large amount of water over a month without making a loud sound. Put a few drops of food coloring in the tank and wait a few minutes without flushing. If color shows up in the bowl, the flapper is leaking.

That is a small repair when caught early. A toilet that hisses or fills for a long time after a flush may have a fill valve problem, and that is a quick one too. If your bill is high and the toilet is the suspect, our leak detection page explains how to check the rest of the house.

Clogged toilets: when a plunger is enough and when it is not

A flat-flange plunger and steady pressure clear most single toilet clogs. If the water in the bowl rises and the plunger does not clear it in a few tries, stop and call. Overuse of a plunger can splash contaminated water and make the clog worse.

If the toilet clogs again soon, or other fixtures gurgle when it flushes, the problem is further down the line. A closet auger reaches clogs a plunger cannot, and a camera shows when the issue is a main line one, see drain cleaning.

Replacing a toilet

A toilet nearing 20 years or with a cracked bowl or tank is a good candidate for replacement. Newer models flush with less water and tend to clog less. A replacement includes removing the old toilet, checking the flange and floor, setting a new wax ring, and testing the flush.

Height, bowl shape, rough-in distance and flush rating are the choices. The rough-in, the distance from the wall to the center of the drain, is usually 12 inches, but it varies, so a quick measurement saves a return trip.

A leak at the base of a toilet that has been going for some time can rot the subfloor. Soft flooring around the toilet is a reason to call soon.

Code points for toilets

Georgia's state minimum plumbing code is the International Plumbing Code (IPC) with Georgia amendments, enforced locally. It sets clearances and connection rules for water closets, which matter most when a toilet is replaced or reset.
