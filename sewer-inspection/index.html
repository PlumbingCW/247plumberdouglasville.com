Sewer Camera Inspection in Douglasville, GA

A waterproof camera on a push cable shows the pipe wall, the joints and the water line, so repairs are based on footage.

Call (770) 214-4545

A sewer camera inspection answers one question that nothing else can: what is actually in the pipe. A technician feeds a small waterproof camera into the line through a cleanout, watches the live feed, and records the run. The footage shows root intrusion, cracks, offsets, bellies and scale, with a locator that marks depth and position above ground.

When a camera inspection is worth booking

  • Before buying a home, especially an older one with a clay or cast iron sewer line.
  • After a backup, to find out why it happened.
  • When a drain clogs repeatedly.
  • Before any digging, so the repair is only as large as it needs to be.
  • To check a line after a repair or after tree removal near it.

If a backup is happening right now, call and we handle it as an emergency first. Our line is open 24/7, and the inspection can follow once the line is clear. For active blockages, see sewer line cleaning.

What the camera can and cannot see

Findings and what they mean

A camera does not see through blocked water or heavy debris, so a line may need clearing before it can be inspected.

How the visit works

From the cleanout to the report

The process takes less time than most people expect.

  1. 1

    Find the cleanout. We locate the access point or discuss adding one if there is none.

  2. 2

    Insert the camera. The camera goes in and travels toward the street or the septic tank.

  3. 3

    Record the run. Findings are noted by distance and the footage is saved.

  4. 4

    Locate problems. The locator marks depth and position of anything that needs attention.

  5. 5

    Review together. You see the footage and get a plain-language summary.

Buying a home near Douglasville

A buyer looking at a home in Douglasville, Lithia Springs or the nearby towns of Austell and Hiram can ask for a camera inspection during the due diligence window. The report gives you something to negotiate with, or confirms the line is sound. Homes with trees close to the line deserve a closer look.

If the house is on septic and not county sewer, a camera can still check the line to the tank, and a separate septic evaluation covers the tank and field. Douglas County's residential plumbing permit application lists both sewer types, so ask the seller which applies.

What a camera report does not replace

Georgia's state minimum plumbing code is the International Plumbing Code (IPC) with Georgia amendments, enforced locally. A camera shows the condition of the pipe, while code compliance is judged against the IPC by the local inspector, so treat the report as evidence and not as an approval.
