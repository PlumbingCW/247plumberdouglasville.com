What hydro jetting does that a snake cannot

A cable drives a hole through a clog and leaves the rest of the buildup on the pipe wall. Hydro jetting sends a nozzle down the line on a high-pressure hose, with water jets that cut grease, scale and soft roots off the wall and flush them downstream. The pipe ends up closer to its original diameter.

That matters when a main line keeps backing up after being snaked. If you are in the middle of a backup now, the line is open 24/7. Keep water use to a minimum, stay out of any sewage and ask us whether jetting or a cable is the better first move.

When a sewage backup is an emergency that calls for jetting

  • Repeated clogs that return within weeks of a cable job.
  • Heavy grease in a kitchen line, common near restaurants and in homes with a lot of cooking.
  • Scale buildup in older cast iron and galvanized drains.
  • Fine root hairs entering a joint, before they become a root mass.
  • A main line that drains slowly but is not fully blocked.

Jetting is not the answer to every backup. A collapsed or heavily cracked pipe can be damaged by high pressure, which is why a camera look comes first on most jobs.

How a jetting visit goes

The sequence keeps the pipe and your yard safe.

  1. 1

    Locate access. A cleanout is found or installed so the hose enters straight into the line.

  2. 2

    Camera first. A camera looks at the pipe so jetting does not push into a broken section.

  3. 3

    Jet from the cleanout. The nozzle works toward the street or the septic tank, loosening buildup as it goes.

  4. 4

    Camera after. A second look confirms the wall is clean and shows any damage the buildup was hiding.

  5. 5

    Explain the result. You get a plain report of what was found and what to do next.

Roots, clay and the Georgia Piedmont

Douglas County sits in Georgia's Piedmont region, where clay soils are common. Clay holds water and shifts as it swells and shrinks, which can pull pipe joints out of alignment, and a small gap is enough to let root hairs in.

Jetting clears the roots and the buildup, but a joint that leaks will let roots back in. If a camera shows a gap, ask about the sewer line repair options, which can stop the cycle.

Commercial kitchens and larger properties

Restaurants and food-service tenants have grease-heavy lines that benefit from regular jetting, including along busy retail corridors such as Douglas Boulevard near Arbor Place. A grease trap does the first filtering, and the line past it still builds up. A scheduled jetting visit is cheaper than a Friday-night overflow, and our commercial plumbing page covers business service in more detail.

Code and cleanout access

Georgia's state minimum plumbing code is the International Plumbing Code (IPC) with Georgia amendments, enforced locally. It requires cleanouts in drain systems, and a jetting visit depends on a usable one, so a missing or buried cleanout is worth fixing.
