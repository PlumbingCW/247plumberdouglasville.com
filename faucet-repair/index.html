Emergency Faucet and Sink Repair in Douglasville, GA

Most faucet problems are small, until a supply valve will not close and water keeps coming. We fix both.

Call (770) 214-4545

A valve that will not shut off is the emergency

A dripping faucet is a nuisance. A supply line that has split under a sink, or an angle stop that will not close, is an emergency. If water is coming from under the sink, close the valves on the supply lines by turning clockwise. If they will not turn, close the main water valve for the house, and call.

The line is open 24/7. Our plumbers can dispatch for a failed valve or a flooding sink at any hour, and replace the shutoff while they are there so the next repair is easier.

Kitchen and bathroom faucets

Most faucets today use a cartridge, a ceramic disc or a ball valve. When one wears out, the faucet drips at the spout, leaks at the base or handle, or becomes stiff. The fix is a cartridge replacement or a new faucet, depending on the make and how easy the parts are to get.

A faucet that is more than 10 to 15 years old, or one with a finish that is flaking, is often better replaced than repaired. A quick conversation about the brand and age tells us which is smarter.

Supply lines and shutoff valves

The braided supply lines under a sink wear out and burst. The angle stop valves that feed them can seize from lack of use and refuse to close in an emergency. Replacing both is a small cost, and it takes the fear out of every future repair.

Look under your sinks. If you see green corrosion, drips or a valve that has never been turned, schedule a swap before the day it matters.

Shower, tub and showerhead leaks

A shower valve that drips at the head, a tub spout that leaks when the shower runs, or a handle that will not stop the water means a worn cartridge or a failing valve body. Some repairs reach the cartridge through the handle. Others need access behind the wall.

A tub drain that is slow is a clog, not a faucet problem. See drain cleaning for that. A bathtub faucet that will not shut off is a repair that should not wait, because it wastes water and can damage the valve further.

Sink drains, traps and disposals

A sink that leaks from under the basin may be a loose slip nut, a cracked trap or a failed disposal flange. A bad connection on a kitchen sink often shows up as a damp cabinet floor, so check the cabinet if you smell damp. For a disposal that hums or leaks, see garbage disposal repair.

Douglas County's residential plumbing permit application lists sinks, lavatories, bathtubs and showers as fee items, which matters for new fixture installs. Straight repairs of existing fixtures usually differ, so ask the county building department about your project.

Code points for faucets and shutoffs

Georgia's state minimum plumbing code is the International Plumbing Code (IPC) with Georgia amendments, enforced locally. It addresses protection against backflow at fixtures, so a faucet or hose connection should use approved parts.
