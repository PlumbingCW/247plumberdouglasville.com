What to do in the first ten minutes of a plumbing emergency

A fast, calm response limits the damage and speeds up the repair. These steps apply to most emergencies, and the 24/7 line is there to walk you through them.

Find the main shutoff

It sits where the water line enters the house, in a basement, crawlspace, garage or utility room. Turn it clockwise until it stops.

Cut power near water

Switch off breakers for any wet area, and do not stand in water to do it. Call 911 if water has reached the panel.

Stop using drains

In a backup, every flush or load of laundry sends more water into a line that cannot take it.

Smell gas? Leave

Get everyone outside and call the gas utility or 911 first. Then call us.

Photograph the damage

Take photos and a short video before you clean up. Your insurer will ask for them.

Call the 24/7 line

Tell the dispatcher what you see and what you have already shut off, and a licensed plumber is sent to your address.

How Douglasville's water, permits and soil shape a plumbing job

Local facts only matter when they change the work. These four do.

The water supplyThe Douglasville-Douglas County Water and Sewer Authority draws Douglas County's drinking water from the Dog River Reservoir and treats it at the Bear Creek plant. Its report lists no hardness figure, so test your tap and flush your water heater once a year.
Permits and inspectionsDouglas County's building department, at 4655 Timber Ridge Drive, takes plumbing permit applications. The form lists water heaters, gas lines, disposals and underground lines, so call before a bigger job.
Sewer or septicThe same permit form separates county sewer from septic tanks, and homes here use both. A backup means one thing on sewer and something else on septic, so tell the dispatcher which you have.
Georgia clayDouglas County sits on the Georgia Piedmont, where clay soil swells when wet and shrinks when dry. That movement strains buried pipe joints, which is why repeat sewer problems deserve a camera inspection.

Along Douglas Boulevard near Arbor Place Mall, restaurants and shops have grease-heavy drains that do better on a jetting schedule than on emergency calls. See commercial plumbing for how that works.

Downtown near the 1956 Douglas County courthouse and O'Neal Plaza, older buildings may have older pipe. If yours does, ask for a camera look before you plan a remodel.

East of town, Lithia Springs and Sweetwater Creek State Park have their own page, plumbing in Lithia Springs, because the area runs under county rather than city government.

Across the county, plumbing work is judged against Georgia's state minimum plumbing code, which is the International Plumbing Code (IPC) with Georgia amendments. Ask any plumber which parts of it apply to your job.
