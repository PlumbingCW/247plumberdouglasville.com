Emergency Garbage Disposal Repair in Douglasville, GA

A jammed or leaking disposal can shut down the kitchen. We diagnose it fast and repair or replace it.

Call (770) 214-4545

A disposal that hums but does not spin, leaks into the cabinet, or leaves the sink backed up is more than an annoyance in a working kitchen. If it is leaking, turn it off at the switch, run no more water into the sink and put a bucket under the leak. The line is open 24/7 for urgent kitchen plumbing.

Never put your hand in a disposal, even when the power is off at the switch. Cut power at the breaker or unplug the unit before any work, and use tongs for anything you can see.

A jam is often a quick fix. A hex wrench in the bottom of the unit turns the flywheel by hand, and the red reset button restores power after a thermal trip. If the problem returns, the motor or bearings may be failing.

What should never go down a disposal

  • Grease, oil and fat, which coat pipes and cool into blockages.
  • Fibrous foods such as celery, corn husks and onion skins in quantity.
  • Coffee grounds in heavy amounts.
  • Bones, pits and fruit stones.
  • Pasta, rice and potato peels, which swell and gum up.
  • Non-food objects.

A disposal is a food waste grinder and not a trash can. Running cold water while it works and for a few seconds after keeps the debris moving. If the drain still backs up, the clog is past the unit and a drain job is called for, see drain cleaning.

Repair or replace a disposal

A disposal that is 8 to 12 years old, or one that leaks from the body, is usually cheaper to replace than to repair. A jam or a loose connection on a newer unit is worth fixing. Horsepower, feed type and noise insulation are the choices when replacing.

Installing a disposal where none existed adds an electrical connection and may require a switch. If a new disposal is part of a kitchen remodel, our plumbing installation page covers the planning side.

What a disposal replacement involves

A swap is a short job when the cabinet is accessible.

  1. 1

    Power off and disconnect. The old unit is unplugged or the breaker turned off, and the drain and dishwasher lines are removed.

  2. 2

    Remove the old unit. The mounting ring is loosened and the unit lifted out.

  3. 3

    Mount the new unit. The flange is reseated with fresh putty and the new unit is attached.

  4. 4

    Connect the lines. Drain, dishwasher knockout and power are connected.

  5. 5

    Test. The unit is run with water and the connections are checked for leaks.

Code points for disposals

Georgia's state minimum plumbing code is the International Plumbing Code (IPC) with Georgia amendments, enforced locally. Its trap and drain rules apply to a kitchen sink with a disposal, and the county permit form lists disposals as an item.
